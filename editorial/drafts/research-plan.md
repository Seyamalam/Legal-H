# Research plan

## Evidence and scope

Inspect DBLA services, forms, the citizen charter, helpline information, and ODR material. Save the page title, publisher, URL, relevant section or page, publication date when available, and access date in the question's source file. Identify the language of the text and mark any translation as a translation. If a page cannot be accessed, record the failure and seek an official alternative without presenting a search snippet as a verified legal text.

The prompt describes recent amendments in 2026. Verify the amending instrument, enactment or publication date, commencement provision, and the corresponding consolidated sections before repeating that claim. Check whether earlier amendments or later notifications explain any difference. Distinguish an enacted rule from a proposal, project activity, or future DLAS feature.

It is crucial to leverage primary sources for legal claims. Secondary reporting can help locate documents or explain a disagreement, but the answer should identify the legal text that controls the issue. Record contrary evidence and differences between versions. Quote sparingly and write findings in our own words; do not republish whole articles or manuals.

For each proposed workflow, trace who makes the decision, what information they receive, how uncertainty appears, and what happens when the process fails. A proposed feature should be labelled as a proposal until evidence shows that the current service provides it.

## Challenge 1: Amendment and digital service delivery

Identify one verified amendment and its precise section. Explain the legal change, the operational consequence, and a digital function that could support it. Check implementation dates, geographic scope, and any notification needed to activate a provision. Avoid attributing an earlier change to a later year.

## Challenge 2: Voice intake and human intervention

Research the current role of the helpline and the authority of legal aid officers and panel lawyers. Distinguish a relative's report from the applicant's instructions or consent. Examine urgent safety escalation, the limits of AI legal information, and how to handle incomplete identity information. Capture the source of each allegation and a safe method for a human to verify it. Do not assume that possession of a phone or knowledge of family details authorizes disclosure.

## Challenge 3: Case prioritization

Carefully identify exactly three factors after conducting a review of the office's responsibilities and any published eligibility or priority criteria. Keep urgent safety needs distinct from legal eligibility and the merits of a claim. Explain how officers see the evidence, uncertainty, and reasons for a suggested priority. Consider missing information, the age of a waiting case, corrections, and whether the mechanism disadvantages people who cannot use digital services.

## Challenge 4: Mediation and ODR

Check the amended legal aid framework, applicable rules and notifications, and current DBLA ODR material. Research maintenance, dowry demands, and domestic abuse separately before deciding which issues can enter mediation. Verify any territorial or subject-matter limits, mandatory pre-case steps, exceptions, and the legal status of an outcome. Distinguish a recorded conversation from a valid agreement. Cover consent, private safety screening, notice, identity, accessible read-back, signatures or permitted alternatives, authentication, record access, failed sessions, and follow-up. Include a refusal condition and a route to human assistance.

## Challenge 5: Restricted information

Choose one item, such as safe-contact instructions, and define who needs access for a specific purpose. Separate the applicant's file from information that a proxy may receive. Research applicable privacy duties before making legal claims. Design access checks, expiry or revocation, an audit trail, and an accessible way for Ripon to complete the same step himself. Explain how a shared phone affects authentication and recovery.

## Challenge 6: A verified application barrier

Inspect the live application path and available official forms without submitting an application or entering real personal data. Record one concrete element and what the evidence shows about whether it is mandatory. Do not assume the form requires an NID, OTP, captcha, upload, or signature without seeing it. Propose one improvement that preserves verified requirements and works through voice when the caller cannot access the internet or read a screen. Test the proposal against the prohibition on unsafe messages to Moyuri's handset.

## Completion checks

Each research file should contain findings, supporting sources, opposing evidence or qualifications, and unresolved points. Each final answer should address every requested element, include useful source attribution within the allowed space, and stay below its word limit with room for differences in word counting. Challenge 4 also needs a simple process diagram and its explanation.

Apply the humanizer pass after checking the evidence. Preserve legal terminology, section numbers, conditions, and uncertainty during editing. Review all six answers together for contradictions about consent, communication, authority, and mediation. Record any office visit or service interaction only if it actually occurred.
