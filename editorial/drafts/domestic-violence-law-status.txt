# Domestic violence law: status check and protection routes

Research date: 5 September 2026. This note supports Challenge 4. It separates the legislation actually inspected from announcements about proposed reform.

## Was the 2010 Act replaced in 2026?

**This research did not verify a gazetted 2026 replacement.** The clearest recent primary evidence is the Ministry of Women and Children Affairs notice dated 1 September 2026, memo 171. It calls an interministerial meeting on 3 September to review the **draft** Domestic Violence Prevention Act, 2026. The attached signed letter was inspected visually. Its description of the measure as a draft makes it unsafe to present a 2026 replacement as established law merely because an earlier announcement used the word “ordinance.” [Ministry notice](https://mowca.gov.bd/pages/notices/আইন-শাখা-পারিবারিক-সহিংসতা-প্রতিরোধ-আইন-২০২৬-এর-খসড়া-পর্যালোচনার-লক্ষ্যে-আন্তঃমন্ত্রণালয়-সভা-আহ্বান-প্রসঙ্গে-১৭১-h3hmbs-6a9685a81f576f3ba28b1560); [signed letter, PDF page 1](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-mowca/2026/8/4fa2e805-6209-498e-89cc-05147e3627d2.pdf).

There is a real discrepancy in official public descriptions. The Information Department's bulletin of 10 February 2026 attributes remarks to the then adviser that refer to a Domestic Violence Prevention Ordinance, 2026. That is evidence of an official announcement, but the retrieved bulletin does not supply a promulgation number, Gazette text, commencement provision or repeal clause. It cannot settle the legal status by itself. [Information Department bulletin, 10 February 2026](https://pressinform.portal.gov.bd/pages/all-notes/%25E0%25A6%25A4%25E0%25A6%25A5%25E0%25A7%258D%25E0%25A6%25AF%25E0%25A6%25AC%25E0%25A6%25BF%25E0%25A6%25AC%25E0%25A6%25B0%25E0%25A6%25A3%25E0%25A7%2580-%25E0%25A7%25A7%25E0%25A7%25A6-%25E0%25A6%25AB%25E0%25A7%2587%25E0%25A6%25AC%25E0%25A7%258D%25E0%25A6%25B0%25E0%25A7%2581%25E0%25A7%259F%25E0%25A6%25BE%25E0%25A6%25B0%25E0%25A6%25BF-%25E0%25A7%25A8%25E0%25A7%25A6%25E0%25A7%25A8%25E0%25A7%25AC-idp063-698afa3624534e63a3cb5b67).

The Legislative and Parliamentary Affairs Division's [2026 ordinances list](https://legislativediv.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/69594f4247dfb83f7508ace1) and [2026 Acts list](https://legislativediv.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/69df4f84e252a315b7e6b8db) were retrieved and searched for the Bengali domestic violence title and its English equivalent. Neither contained a matching entry. Targeted searches of the Bangladesh Gazette, Laws of Bangladesh and Legislative Division domains also did not identify the replacement instrument. A missing index result does not prove that no instrument exists. The notice and index results support a cautious drafting decision, not a complete legislative-history conclusion.

The Ministry continues to provide the [2010 Act on its laws page](https://mowca.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/694032f035ce18e1c05647bd). That page's content update is 1 May 2025, so a newer site-wide footer must not be used as proof that its legal content was reviewed in September 2026. The original Gazette was independently located through [Bangladesh Government Press's publication record](https://www.dpp.gov.bd/bgpress/index.php/document/get_extraordinary/9904).

**Recommended treatment in the answer:** cite the protection provisions of the 2010 Act as the verified statutory text, disclose that 2026 replacement status remains unconfirmed, and require the legal aid officer to check the operative instrument before filing. Do not describe the 2026 proposal as law in force, or say conclusively that it never became law. The specific unresolved evidence is a promulgated 2026 Gazette, together with its commencement, repeal and transitional provisions, if one exists. The result of the meeting scheduled for 3 September was not located.

## Protection and proxy application under the verified 2010 text

Source for this section: *Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act, 2010*, Act 58 of 2010, Gazette published 12 October 2010. The Ministry-hosted original Gazette was downloaded and the relevant Bengali pages were checked visually. The following are paraphrases of public statutory text, not quotations. [Original Gazette PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-mowca/2024/12/2929424a13a2431881552e94767678dd.pdf); [Government Press copy](https://www.dpp.gov.bd/upload_file/gazettes/9904_56254.pdf).

| Issue | Verified provision | Consequence for Moyuri and Ripon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who may apply | Section 11(1), PDF page 7, printed page 9381: the aggrieved person, or an enforcement officer, service provider or any other person on her behalf may apply for relief. Section 11(2) requires the prescribed form. | A trusted cousin can potentially help bring a protection application on her behalf. Ripon does not have to claim to be the victim. Standing to apply does not make him the decision maker on settlement or disclosure. |
| First hearing step | Section 11(3), PDF page 8: the court must fix a hearing date within seven working days of receiving the application. | This is a duty to fix the hearing date, not a promise of a final protection order within seven days. |
| Place of application | Section 12, PDF page 8: jurisdiction includes where the applicant resides, the respondent resides, the violence occurred, or the victim temporarily resides. | Temporary safe residence may matter to venue. A lawyer must check the actual facts and competent court. |
| Urgent interim relief | Section 13(1), PDF page 8: if the court is satisfied from the material supplied that domestic violence has occurred or is likely, it may issue an interim protection order without first hearing the respondent and direct a response within seven working days of notice. | Immediate protection need not wait for a joint online mediation session. The court decides whether the evidence meets the statutory condition. |
| Protection and contact restrictions | Section 14, PDF pages 8 and 9: after giving both parties an opportunity to be heard, the court may prohibit violence, assistance or encouragement of violence, entry to places the victim frequents and contact through personal, written, telephone, mobile, email or other means. It can also address violence against helpers and dependants. | A safety assessment should consider both Moyuri and the person helping her. A private video call cannot itself give these court orders. |
| Housing and documents | Section 15(1), PDF page 9: residence orders can address exclusion or eviction, alternative accommodation or rent, and safe shelter under the enforcement officer's supervision with the victim's consent. Paragraph (e) permits an order allowing her to enter the shared residence with that officer to collect personal belongings and documents. | Her documents being locked away is relevant to a possible court remedy. Do not tell her to return alone or retrieve them secretly. The provision covers documents broadly; it does not name NID specifically. |
| Maintenance alongside protection | Section 16(5) and (6), PDF page 11, printed page 9385: the court may order adequate and reasonable maintenance for the victim and her child, including a lump sum or monthly payment. | Maintenance should not be described as available only through the Family Court. This route exists in the inspected protection statute too. The lawyer should coordinate claims and orders. |
| Court | Section 21(1), PDF page 13, printed page 9387: applications, offences and proceedings under the Act are dealt with by a Judicial Magistrate or, where applicable, Metropolitan Magistrate. | A DBLA mediation worker should arrange legal referral; the worker does not issue protection or residence orders. |

Sections 8 and 9, PDF page 7, also provide for shelter and medical assistance requested by the victim or, on her behalf, a police officer, enforcement officer, service provider or another person. These provisions do not establish which local facility currently has space or the practical response time. Those operational questions need an actual referral check.

## How to use the nuance

Separate three decisions: whether Ripon can bring information or an application on Moyuri's behalf; which protective remedies a lawyer should seek; and whether Moyuri freely wants any proposed mediation. The first does not decide the others. Section 11's broad standing must not be rewritten as a general power for a relative to waive her rights, settle her maintenance or expose her location. That last point is a service-design and consent safeguard, not a quotation from section 11.

The answer can recommend urgent legal assessment of a protection application, safe shelter and medical needs without pretending that the available facts prove a particular criminal charge. Violence safety screening, lawful maintenance remedies and the dowry-related offences should remain distinct in the intake and referral process.
