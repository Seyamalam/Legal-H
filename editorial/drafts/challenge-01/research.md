# Research findings for challenge 1

Reviewed on 5 September 2026. The legal findings below rely on official documents; the proposed workflow is our design recommendation.

## Recommended legal change

Use section 16(1) of the Legal Aid Services Act, 2000, as substituted by section 24 of the Legal Aid Services (Amendment) Act, 2026, Act No. 45. It expressly permits applications directly and online to the Supreme Court legal aid office under the Directorate or a regional, district, or upazila legal aid office. This is a precise digital-service provision and gives a stronger answer to this question than a broad statement that technology can improve mediation. [C1-S1 and C1-S2](sources.md)

The official 2014 English version sent applications to the Supreme Court, District, or Special Committee without expressly naming an online route in section 16. The defensible comparison is the express wording and office-based route. The evidence does not show that the 2026 Act launched the existing web portal or that earlier online applications were invalid. [C1-S3 and C1-S4](sources.md)

## Dates and section references

The 2026 Act received assent and publication on 10 April 2026. Its section 1(3) brings provisions other than those reserved by section 1(2) into force immediately. The reserved provisions concern sections 21B and 21C and the Schedule; they do not include replacement of section 16. The consolidated PDF's 4 May print date and the laws index upload date are not the amendment's enactment date. [C1-S1 and C1-S2](sources.md)

Use both references accurately: principal Act section 16(1); amending Act section 24. Bangla section ১৬ is section 16. Do not cite section 24 of the principal Act, which deals with regulations.

The 2026 Act also replaces the rejection-appeal route: section 16(2) now names the Director General and retains a 60-day period. Older official descriptions may still name the Board. That is a useful supporting finding for service design, but the 200-word answer should concentrate on one change. [C1-S1 to C1-S3](sources.md)

## Operational consequence

A legal aid office needs a process for receiving an application, preserving the supplied information, recording when and where it arrived, and putting it before an authorized officer. Digital intake can reduce the need to travel to the district town merely to start the process. None of this automatically waives identity, evidence, eligibility, or valid authorization requirements. The statute authorizes an online route; it does not require applicants to use that route exclusively. [C1-S1, C1-S2, C1-S4](sources.md)

For Moyuri, a web form alone would leave the main access problems intact. She lacks safe phone control, internet experience, and access to her NID. Ripon can report facts by voice but cannot safely speak as if he were Moyuri. The design should allow him to start a provisional record containing his report and his own contact role. It should clearly distinguish that record from a legally complete application or Moyuri's verified instructions. These are inferences from the supplied scenario and proposed safeguards, not a description of existing DBLA software.

## Proposed digital support

A Bangla voice intake route could map Ripon's answers to the same fields used by the legal aid office. It should read the answers back, allow corrections, mark missing facts, and give an officer a source-labelled draft. A person should determine what further documents and authorization are needed, then record acceptance or rejection through the official procedure. AI may assist with transcription and routing; it should not grant legal aid or treat a relative's statement as verified consent.

The proposed service should retain direct filing and assisted entry. A case reference should be available through a caller-initiated voice route; it should not trigger an SMS to Moyuri. An officer should arrange any contact with Moyuri through a method she can safely authorize. The evidence establishes an online form today, while this combined voice workflow remains a proposal. [C1-S4](sources.md)

## Alternatives considered

Section 2(ড) recognizes physical or virtual attendance in mediation; section 21C gives a properly executed and certified settlement legal force. Both support digital services, but they require more qualification about commencement and formalities. Section 7(1)(গ) expressly addresses emergency legal support. These are useful for later challenges. Section 16(1) most directly answers the citizen-access question within 200 words. [C1-S1 and C1-S2](sources.md)

## Limits and checks before implementation

This research did not verify that every named type of office currently exists in every locality or accepts the same online channel. The legal permission does not prove a uniform operational rollout. No real application was submitted, no claimant identity was entered, and no district-office visit took place.
