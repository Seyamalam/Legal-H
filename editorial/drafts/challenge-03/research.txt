# Research notes: fair case prioritization

Research checked on 5 September 2026. The proposed mechanism helps the DLAO decide which file needs attention next. It does not decide guilt, legal-aid eligibility or the eventual merits of litigation.

## Evidence and legal boundaries

The current Legal Aid Services Act recognizes emergency legal support and digital access with database protection. Section 16(1) expressly permits direct or online applications to the specified legal aid offices. Section 16(2) allows an applicant whose request is rejected to appeal to the Director General within 60 days. This statutory rejection appeal should not be confused with the proposed service review of queue priority. [S1](sources.md#s1-current-legal-aid-act)

The UN essential-services guidance supports prompt attention to reported violence, accessible initial contact and continuing review of risk. These principles inform the design; they are not a statutory Bangladesh formula for ranking all legal-aid files. [S2](sources.md#s2-un-essential-services-package)

NIST AI RMF 1.0 calls for documented responsibilities for human oversight, evaluation of fairness and bias, feedback and appeal mechanisms, and monitoring after deployment. Its human-AI appendix warns that officials also bring biases and may defer too readily to opaque outputs. A person clicking "approve" therefore does not demonstrate meaningful review. [S3](sources.md#s3-nist-ai-risk-management-framework)

## Use exactly three factors

| Factor | What the officer assesses | Digital representation | Application to Moyuri |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Risk of harm if action waits | Present danger, recent or worsening violence, threats, serious injury, child danger and urgent loss of basic safety | Structured yes/no/unknown fields with incident time, short narrative, source and reason for an alert; a proposed immediate-review flag overrides the routine queue | Ripon reports repeated assaults and deprivation; present danger remains unknown. Prompt human safety review is warranted, with stronger escalation if acute danger emerges. |
| 2. Time-sensitive legal need and an arguable route to relief | A legal deadline, hearing, threatened eviction or other consequence of delay; whether an available legal remedy or referral needs prompt assessment | Human-confirmed issue tags, date and source of each deadline, proposed remedy and an "assessment pending" field. Show the reason and timing, without a predicted win percentage. | A lawyer should assess protection and financial-support options. The intake does not establish a filing deadline, her wishes, all facts, or the exact applicable procedure. |
| 3. Vulnerability and practical ability to obtain help | How poverty, dependency, disability, care responsibilities, restricted movement or unsafe communication prevent timely access | Record concrete barriers and requested accommodations separately for applicant and helper; identify safe channels and unresolved needs | Moyuri lacks safe phone access, access to her NID and independent travel. Ripon's blindness requires spoken assistance. Neither should reduce the file's priority. |

The second factor addresses legal merit through a provisional human assessment of a plausible need for relief. It avoids claiming that every allegation is legally established. The first factor remains independent: uncertainty about the final legal claim must not suppress a reported safety concern.

These are design choices, not an existing statutory scoring test. Do not add evidence completeness, social status, applicant persistence or likelihood of a quick settlement as hidden fourth factors.

## Proposed comparison mechanism

Use a versioned decision table with a small number of priority bands, such as immediate human review, prompt review and routine review. Show each factor separately. A single unexplained score hides whether a case ranked low because its risk was assessed or because the applicant could not answer a question.

The system may flag a reason for review, group comparable cases and show approaching deadlines. The DLAO confirms or changes priority and records a short explanation. The interface must retain the original account beside the summary, identify unverified facts, and allow the officer to correct a transcription without losing its source. Never label an AI summary as a witness statement or legal finding.

A serious safety flag bypasses the ordinary list. For otherwise comparable files, use time since first contact as an administrative tie-breaker and show how long each has waited. Do not reset that clock when missing documents arrive. Review ageing files and newly reported risks daily so that routine cases cannot disappear indefinitely. These queue controls are safeguards supporting the three factors, not additional assessments of merit.

Set response targets only after the office tests staff capacity. The design should record a responsible officer, an acknowledgement, a due time, backup cover and an overdue escalation route. Avoid describing proposed same-day handling as a current service guarantee or a statutory deadline.

## The strongest protection against exclusion

Create an assisted-review route that preserves the first-contact date. Store a missing answer as "unknown" with the reason if supplied, never zero risk, no merit or a failed eligibility check. A human helps complete the record through an accessible, safe channel. The system cannot automatically reject, downgrade or close a file because an NID, upload, OTP, signature or direct survivor account is missing.

Separate urgency from evidence confidence. For example, a report of a weapon threat can warrant immediate review even when it comes from a brother and requires verification. Lower confidence should prompt a proportionate verification step; it should not mathematically cancel a serious reported hazard. Do not ask Moyuri to obtain documents from Sohel or unsafe evidence merely to improve a score.

This does not remove lawful filing or eligibility requirements. An officer may need to verify identity, representation and other requirements before a particular formal act. Record that procedural work separately while providing the intake and safeguarding response appropriate to the report.

## Make transparency usable

Give applicants a brief explanation of the reason for their priority, next step and expected contact method in spoken Bangla or another accessible format. Reveal only information safe for that recipient. Ripon's role as reporter does not automatically entitle him to Moyuri's full case record.

Offer a simple request for reconsideration of priority through the same assisted channels, with a different authorized reviewer where practical. Distinguish this proposed internal process from the Act's appeal against rejection of aid. Keep both available explanations accurate; do not imply that a queue review extends a legal appeal deadline.

Publish the criteria and aggregate service results, not names or sensitive case details. A plain-language rule such as "reported immediate danger receives immediate staff review" is more useful than publishing model weights that applicants cannot interpret.

## Test for fairness and actual review

Before launch, compare fictional cases that differ only in channel, accent, disability accommodation, missing NID or clarity of narration. Equivalent needs should receive equivalent access to review. Test abrupt disconnections and negative Bangla phrases because transcription errors can reverse a risk answer.

Audit waiting time, missed urgent cases, abandoned intake, refusals, correction requests and officer overrides by channel and relevant access barriers. Collect only the attributes needed for that assessment; small-group public statistics can identify people. Examine whether prioritizing severe cases indefinitely excludes other applicants, and adjust capacity and ageing reviews rather than quietly lowering reported risk.

Avoid training a ranking model simply on past approvals, closures or settlements. Such records can reflect earlier exclusion, differences in access to counsel, missing follow-up or pressure to settle. NIST's bias guidance recognizes organizational, statistical and human sources of bias. [S4](sources.md#s4-nist-bias-guidance)

Have trained staff examine a sample of lower-priority files, not just the files the tool labels urgent. Record why officers override suggestions and whether the override improved handling. A supervisor should review unexplained downgrades. That is especially relevant where a polished screen may make an uncertain output seem authoritative.

## Nuance and unresolved policy choices

Priority is relative to the files waiting at that time. The case study supports prompt review for Moyuri; it does not prove that her file outranks every other file in Joypurhat. Another applicant may face an immediate lethal threat or an expiring court deadline.

A survivor's practical barriers are relevant because they affect access and the consequences of delay. Disability, gender or poverty must not become assumptions about credibility, capacity or legal worth. Store the actual access need and confirm it with the person when safe.

The office must approve thresholds, staffing, override authority and review intervals. Current eligibility rules, proxy requirements and any mandatory mediation steps require legal verification for the particular claim and district. The system must not equate willingness to mediate or a predicted quick settlement with greater merit, or itself penalize refusal. A lawyer must assess any actual legal consequence under current rules.
